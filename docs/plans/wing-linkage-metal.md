# Machined swing linkage (`wing-linkage-metal`)

> **Status:** SUPERSEDED 2026-09-29 by `righting-design.md` (user: too many
> machined parts). Kept as the record of the load-path reasoning. CONCEPT in CAD. Nothing machined. Generated by
> `aow_sim.cad_wing_linkage_metal` into `aow-bike` → `wing-linkage-metal`.
> Picks up `righting-linkage-margin.md` step 5 for the diamond only; the
> linkage choice itself is still open.

The brief (user): the diamond swing linkage built to take the hardest hits on
the bike: XC330-driven, **no pin loads reaching the XC330**, every pivot in a
bearing surface (reamed Delrin), machined on the CNC mill and lathe, with extra
beef.

## Decisions

- **The diamond, scaled 1.25×** (`config/swing_linkage_shared_rod.yaml` about
  the rod). Scaling all four lengths keeps every angle, so the stroke's
  current is unchanged. The planar scorer gives 586 counts at 1.0, 1.15, 1.25,
  1.35 and 1.5, with the panel re-derived onto the same line each time. What
  it buys is room for walls, plus pin forces at 1/scale. What it costs is the
  servo shaft height (10.1 → 15.1 mm above the axle) and the panel standoff
  (19.9 → 15.5; the coupler needs 8). **Not re-run in the sim at 1.25**, only
  the planar scorer.
- **A supported crankshaft.** Two identical half-cranks (turned Φ8 journal,
  milled web, Φ12 shoulder bearing on the bushing flange) and a pressed Φ5
  steel crankpin. Each journal runs in a flanged Delrin bushing in its own
  bulkhead. Both couplers sit side by side on the pin between the webs.
- **XC330 → crank through an Oldham coupling** with a Delrin disc: a horn
  hub (4 × M2 on the horn), a disc with a tongue on each face at 90°, and the
  journal's end slotted. It carries torque only; radial and axial float mean
  no pin force reaches the servo's output bearing.
  - **What it does NOT remove is torque.** A knock that twists a wing about
    the rod still goes wing → coupler → crank → Oldham → gearbox. Only a hard
    stop or a slip clutch would stop that; neither is in the concept.
- **Each rocker arm shares a plane with a crank web.** A pin joint needs its
  two links in different planes. The only planes free for the rocker arms are
  the webs', and they clear: 4.3 mm at the worst point of the stroke (±56%),
  2D and then 3D.
- **Both wings on one fixed Φ6 steel rod,** on long Delrin-bushed hubs (13 mm
  per rocker).
  - Each wing also has a **second knuckle** on the rod beyond the far
    bulkhead: wing R's ahead of the front one, wing L's behind the rear one,
    under the servo.
  - So each panel is held at both ends, and a force (as opposed to a torque)
    on a panel goes rod → bulkheads.
- **L/R pairs are one part.** The stack is symmetric about the mid-plane and
  the rest pose about X = 0. So the half-cranks, couplers, rockers, knuckles,
  bulkheads and every bushing are each one part, the other side being the
  same solid turned 180° about Z. `--check` confirms the volumes match.
- **Frame:** two Al bulkheads (8 mm) and a bridge plate on top. The bridge's
  top face is the mount to the bike. A printed cradle hangs the XC330 by its
  back face and leaves the horn face free.

## Open, and what this does not check

- **Mass.** 298 g against the sim's whole bike at 1.02 kg. The bridge (≈45 g),
  the bulkheads (≈34 g each) and the rockers (≈30 g each) are where it is.
  None of them is pocketed yet. Until it is lightened or the sim is given the
  mass, the righting numbers and every policy assume a much lighter linkage.
- **Not checked against the rest of the bike.** This is the module alone: no
  drive, no wheels, no chassis. Packing under the drive was a separate rough
  2D check (drive at 45°, 1.0× diamond), not repeated at 1.25×.
- **Assumed, not verified:** that the XC330's 16 × 30 hole pattern is on its
  back face; that the servo is fine hanging by that face alone.
- **Not modelled:** screws (bridge, panel pads, Oldham hub), rod retention
  clips, running clearances (everything is drawn line-to-line, so the fits are
  a machining call), and the wire exit from the XC330.
- **The panels are placeholders** on the linkage config's line, 6 mm like the
  sim's.
